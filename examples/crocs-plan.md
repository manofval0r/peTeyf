# Executive summary

Crocs is one of the most recognisable comfort brands in the world. That does not automatically make it a recurring-revenue business. Most customers buy when they need a fresh pair, then disappear until the next practical need or collaboration catches their eye.

Crocs Club closes that gap. The proposed membership gives customers one curated pair each quarter, early access to selected drops, member-only Jibbitz packs, and a direct voice in future colourways. The point is not to push more shoes. It is to make self-expression feel fresh often enough that returning becomes natural.

```metrics
Year-one members | 340k | target
Quarterly member revenue | $42m | target
Acquisition cost | $31 | target
Quarterly churn | 8% | ceiling
Net promoter score | 72 | target
```

> [!KEY] The strategic choice
> Build the programme around personal expression, not replenishment. Comfort gets the first purchase. A steady stream of small, distinctive choices earns the next one.

# Where we are today

Crocs already has the ingredients of a membership brand: unmistakable product design, broad awareness, collectable collaborations, and a customization system customers understand instantly. The weakness is continuity. Direct-to-consumer channels capture strong margins, while wholesale creates reach, but neither gives buyers a consistent reason to return between product moments.

The near-term job is simple. Start with people who already own several pairs, learn what keeps them engaged, and prove retention before spending heavily on new audiences.

# The opportunity

Comfort footwear has held on to the habits formed during the early 2020s. At the same time, younger buyers increasingly treat footwear as an affordable way to signal taste, fandom, and identity. Crocs sits at the meeting point of those two behaviours.

The proposed programme should therefore behave less like a shoe subscription and more like a seasonal creative drop. Members know when something new is coming, but the colour, collaboration, or charm pack still carries surprise.

# Strategy

## Positioning

Crocs Club is the easiest way for people who already love comfort and customization to keep their rotation feeling personal. Every quarter brings a considered product moment, not a random box of inventory.

## Audience sequence

- **Collectors and repeat buyers (40%)** already understand the product and give the fastest signal on retention.
- **Gift buyers (30%)** are less price-sensitive and give the programme a strong holiday use case.
- **Style-led digital buyers (30%)** provide the largest growth pool once onboarding and curation are proven.

## Offer design

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Product | Club members receive one curated pair per quarter plus a member-only Jibbitz pack |
| Choice | Members select a style lane, size, colour appetite, and collaboration interests |
| Price | $49 per quarter for the core tier; no launch discount |
| Added value | Early access, pause controls, easy exchanges, and member voting |
| Channels | Website first, app for management, retail stores for recruitment and fitting |
| Promotion | Existing customers first; creators and paid reach only after retention is visible |

# Four-quarter action plan

## Q1: Build the foundation

- Create the member proposition, preference quiz, and pause controls.
- Recruit a 5,000-person founding cohort from high-frequency direct buyers.
- Produce a product and content library built around customization stories.

> [!ACTION] First decision gate
> The Growth Lead reviews activation, preference completion, and first-quarter satisfaction before the public launch receives paid media.

## Q2: Launch and learn

- Open Crocs Club to the existing email and SMS audience.
- Introduce a two-sided referral offer based on exclusive product, not a price cut.
- Test three curation lanes: expressive, classic, and collaboration-led.

## Q3: Scale what retains

- Move paid investment to the two creative ideas with the strongest retained-member economics.
- Add corporate and group gifting for the holiday pipeline.
- Launch pause, swap, and win-back journeys before increasing reach.

## Q4: Turn membership into culture

- Release the first member-voted colourway.
- Host a small member event around customization and collaboration previews.
- Publish a short “Year in Comfort” recap using real member choices and product data.

# Controls

## Monthly view

Track active members, quarter-two retention, acquisition cost, gross margin, exchange rate, and net promoter score. A metric outside tolerance receives a written owner and recovery date within two weeks.

## Decision rhythm

| Frequency | Forum | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Growth stand-up | Creative, operations, and service fixes |
| Monthly | Performance review | Channel budget moves up to 10% |
| Quarterly | Strategy review | Pricing, tier design, and audience order |

> [!WARNING] Stop-and-fix threshold
> If quarterly churn rises above 12% in two consecutive cohorts, pause paid acquisition and repair onboarding, curation, and the first delivery before scaling again.

# Risks and responses

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collaboration fatigue | Medium | Medium | Cap the calendar and reserve the strongest moments for members |
| Acquisition costs rise | High | Medium | Prioritise referrals, retail recruitment, and owned audiences |
| Supply costs increase | Medium | High | Use forward commitments and quarterly price reviews |
| Curation disappoints | Medium | High | Improve the preference quiz, exchanges, and member voting |
| Churn exceeds target | Medium | High | Offer pause and swap controls before a cancellation path |
