# Executive summary

Brew & Bloom is a speciality coffee roaster with two cafés and a growing
wholesale account base. The business has earned a strong local reputation but
has not yet converted that reputation into recurring revenue. This plan sets out
how we will launch and scale a **coffee subscription** over the next twelve
months, moving from one-off purchases to predictable monthly income.

The opportunity is clear. Subscription coffee is growing faster than the wider
speciality market, and our existing customers already buy from us every week.
The constraint is not demand — it is that we have never asked for the second
purchase in a structured way.

```metrics
Monthly recurring revenue | £18,400 | target
Active subscribers | 640 | target
Customer acquisition cost | £24 | -32%
Average order value | £31 | +14%
Churn (monthly) | 4.2% | -1.8pts
```

> [!KEY] The one-sentence strategy
> Sell the habit, not the bag: position the subscription as a monthly ritual
> that removes a small decision from the customer's week.

## Where we are today

Two cafés generate steady footfall but thin margins. Wholesale is profitable but
concentrated in four accounts, which is a risk. Online sales exist but are
treated as an afterthought: a single product page, no email capture, and no
reason to return.

# Current marketing situation

## Market analysis

The UK speciality coffee market continues to premiumise. Consumers are
increasingly comfortable paying for provenance, and the at-home brewing
category has held onto the habits formed when cafés were closed. Subscription
boxes have moved from novelty to routine for a meaningful segment of urban
professionals.

```chart line
title: At-home speciality coffee spend index
2022 | 100
2023 | 112
2024 | 121
2025 | 134
2026 | 148
```

The trend matters because it shifts the question from *"can we sell more coffee?"*
to *"who will let us choose their coffee for them every month?"*

## Competitor analysis

| Competitor | Positioning | Price / month | Weakness we can exploit |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Box Co. | Convenience, wide choice | £18 | Generic beans, no local story |
| Grind House Club | Third-wave credibility | £28 | Poor onboarding, high churn |
| Supermarket own-brand | Price | £9 | No freshness, no ritual |
| **Brew & Bloom** | **Local craft, monthly ritual** | **£24** | **Small brand awareness** |

Our advantage is not price. It is proximity, provenance, and the fact that a
real person roasts the coffee and writes the tasting note.

## Customer analysis

Three segments matter:

1. **The local regular (43%)** — already visits weekly, high trust, low
   acquisition cost. The easiest first subscriber.
2. **The gift buyer (31%)** — seasonal, price-insensitive, buys for others.
   Strong in November and December.
3. **The remote professional (26%)** — wants convenience and quality, discovers
   us through search and social, most likely to churn if the first box disappoints.

# SWOT and issues

## SWOT

```swot
Strengths | Roastery ownership and freshness; Strong repeat footfall; Authentic origin story
Weaknesses | Low brand awareness outside the city; No email list or CRM discipline; Single-product website
Opportunities | Subscription growth in at-home brewing; Corporate gifting in Q4; Wholesale expansion into offices
```

## Key issues

> [!INSIGHT] The central issue
> We have earned trust in person and have no mechanism to carry that trust
> online. Every week we serve customers we cannot reach again.

# Marketing strategy

## Positioning

For urban professionals who care about what they drink but do not want to
research it, **Brew & Bloom is the local roaster that chooses your coffee for
you** — because a monthly ritual should feel handled, not homework.

## Targeting

We will sequence the segments rather than serve them all at once. The local
regular validates the product and generates testimonials; the gift buyer funds
Q4 acquisition; the remote professional becomes viable only once retention is
proven.

![A flat lay of coffee beans, a grinder and a brewing scale](search:coffee beans grinder flat lay)

## The marketing mix

### Product

Three tiers, differentiated by cadence and curation rather than volume:

- **The Weekly** — 250 g, one origin, £12
- **The Ritual** — 500 g, two origins, £24 *(hero product)*
- **The Cellar** — 1 kg, rotating reserve, £46

### Price

Priced at a premium to supermarket coffee and slightly below third-wave
competitors. No launch discount; instead, the first month includes a free
brew guide, which protects perceived value.

### Place

The website becomes the primary conversion surface. Cafés act as physical
proof and referral points; wholesale becomes a corporate subscription channel.

### Promotion

Earned media and email first, paid social second. We will invest in the
story — the farm, the roast, the person — because that is the only story a
national competitor cannot copy.

# Action programs

## Q1 — Foundations

- Rebuild the subscription landing page around a single call to action.
- Instrument email capture in both cafés with a QR code and a small incentive.
- Photograph the roastery and the founding story for reuse across channels.

> [!ACTION] Owner and date
> Owner: Marketing lead. First review: end of January.

## Q2 — Launch and learn

- Launch the three tiers to the existing email list before any paid spend.
- Run a referral programme: one free box for the referrer, one for the friend.
- Begin a fortnightly "roast note" email, written by the roaster.

## Q3 — Scale what works

- Move paid social budget to the two best-performing creative concepts.
- Open a corporate gifting page for Q4 pipeline.
- Introduce a win-back flow at the point of cancellation.

## Q4 — Gifting and retention

- Launch the gift subscription with a printed card.
- Run a subscriber-only tasting evening in the café.
- Publish a transparent "year in coffee" report as a brand asset.

# Controls

## Measurement

We will review a single dashboard monthly against five numbers: MRR, active
subscribers, churn, CAC, and gross margin. Anything outside tolerance triggers a
written review within two weeks.

## Review cadence

| Cadence | Forum | Decision rights |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Marketing stand-up | Creative and channel tactics |
| Monthly | Performance review | Budget reallocation up to 10% |
| Quarterly | Strategy review | Pricing, tiers, and segment focus |

> [!WARNING] Trigger for a rethink
> If monthly churn exceeds 6% for two consecutive months, we pause paid
> acquisition until the onboarding and first-box experience is fixed.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green bean price spike | Medium | High | Forward contracts; review pricing quarterly |
| Delivery cost inflation | High | Medium | Minimum order value; local pickup option |
| Churn above target | Medium | High | Onboarding call; win-back flow; pause option |
| Brand awareness stays low | Medium | Medium | Partnership-led reach; local press |
